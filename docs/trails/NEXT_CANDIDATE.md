# Next candidate: the waste that came back

Researched 2026-09-22. Candidate only; no reader content or route data has been added.

**Teaching question:** When an exporter calls a shipment “recyclable,” who checks what is inside—and who pays when it must come back?

**Recommendation: prioritize the 2019 return of Canadian waste from the Philippines for the next research packet. Verdict: needs targeted research before publishing a complete physical trail.** Its documented consolidation, international return and intended disposal offer meaningful handoffs. It also exposes a change of denominator: the returned 69 containers were a subset of 103 originally imported. This is a specific case, not a reconstruction from national trade totals. Brazil–China soy remains in the backlog; this review did not research that separate candidate.

## Candidate stop sequence

| Stop | What the evidence supports | Continuity and display limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Manila International Container Port, Philippines | Two remaining containers were transferred to Subic shortly before the return operation; the Department of Finance records inspections at both ports in early May 2019. [S1, S2] | This stage concerns **two**, not all 69. It should be a branch joining the main group, or an explicitly scoped introductory record. Do not draw every returned container through Manila on this evidence alone. |
| Subic New Container Terminal, Philippines | The two joined 67 already at Subic. All 69 were loaded on **MV Bavaria** overnight May 30–31, 2019; departure followed on May 31. [S2] | Strongest physical handoff: named terminal, vessel, dates and count. The reported loading operation is backed by a contemporaneous official statement and the agency reporter’s departure photograph. |
| Deltaport / Port of Vancouver, Canada | A July 1 Philippine government news report describes the 69 arriving June 29 on **Anna Maersk**. [S3] | This article explicitly relies on reports of arrival. Obtain a receiving-port, carrier or ECCC record before treating this as independently primary-verified. The changed vessel requires a transshipment record; do not render Subic–Canada as one uninterrupted ship journey. |
| Metro Vancouver Waste-to-Energy Facility, Burnaby, Canada | The disposal authority’s July 2019 director newsletter confirms the facility was selected for the returned waste; the operator confirms its Burnaby location. [S4, S6] | Selection is not a completion certificate. The finale may currently describe the **planned destination**, not assert all 69 loads were received and incinerated. No shipment-specific electricity output is established. |

Use three principal stops—Subic, Canadian arrival, Burnaby—only after the two Canadian handoffs are verified. Add Manila as the two-container branch. Add an intermediate transfer port only if a primary record identifies the transfer. An unsourced Taiwan waypoint would make the map look more complete while weakening its evidence.

## Source register and locators

- **S1 — Philippine Department of Finance, May 7, 2019:** [PHL ready to re-export waste to Canada by May 15](https://www.dof.gov.ph/phl-ready-to-re-export-waste-to-canada-by-may-15/). Locate the paragraphs beginning “Guerrero said a series of meetings” and “The 69 container vans.” Establishes inspections at Manila/Subic and the relationship between 69 remaining and 103 original containers. This is an official account of preparations, not proof that a projected departure occurred.
- **S2 — Philippine News Agency, May 31, 2019:** [Canadian garbage finally out of Subic](https://www.pna.gov.ph/articles/1071200). Locate the paragraphs beginning “Eisma said a total of 69,” “SBMA documented,” and “The loading.” Agency reporting with direct SBMA attribution establishes 67-plus-two consolidation, loading and departure. A direct-page fetch failed, but the indexed article text and caption were readable; retain that access limitation for independent review.
- **S3 — Philippine News Agency, July 1, 2019:** [PH-Canada ‘cordial’ ties back after return of garbage](https://www.pna.gov.ph/articles/1073698). Locate “Reports showed” and the preceding paragraph about June 29. Useful receiving-event lead; government hosting does not turn its report-based arrival claim into a port receipt.
- **S4 — Jen McCutcheon, Metro Vancouver director, July 2019:** [Director’s Update](https://jenmccutcheon.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/eaa-july-2019-directors-update-erl-final-2.pdf), **page 4**, “Metro Vancouver Provides Safe Disposal of Canadian Waste from the Philippines.” Primary public communication by a disposal-authority board member, hosted on her website; text extraction checked. Confirms the selected facility and reports 1,500 tonnes; it provides neither weighbridge records nor a dated completion statement.
- **S5 — Environment and Climate Change Canada, May 22, 2019:** [Canada takes next step to bring abandoned waste back from the Philippines](https://www.canada.ca/en/environment-climate-change/news/2019/05/canada-takes-next-step-to-bring-abandoned-waste-back-from-the-philippines.html), opening announcement and **Quick facts**. Confirms the repatriation contract with Bolloré Logistics Canada and Canadian government responsibility for preparation, transfer, shipping and disposal costs. Records the mixed contents and distinguishes Canadian export rules then from Philippine import restrictions. It reports a 2016 court return order; the underlying judgment has not been checked here.

- **S6 — Metro Vancouver, accessed September 22, 2026:** [About Waste-to-Energy Facility](https://metrovancouver.org/services/solid-waste/about-waste-to-energy-facility), opening paragraph and **Location**. Establishes that the facility has operated in Burnaby since 1988 and locates it at 5150 Riverbend Drive. Current annual capacity/output figures are context, not measurements of the 2019 consignment.

Avoid calling every shipment “toxic,” claiming criminal guilt, or naming a private actor as knowingly fraudulent. Attribute prohibited-import and return-order claims to ECCC unless the underlying records are obtained. Do not extend the export-rules summary from 2013–2016 into advice on present law.

## Exact next research assignment

1. Find an ECCC, carrier or receiving-port record for the June 2019 return, including any **MV Bavaria → Anna Maersk** transfer and the location/date of that handoff. Container identifiers would provide the best continuity check.
2. Find Metro Vancouver’s receiving/disposal completion record and actual mass. Preserve partial receipts or staged treatment; do not turn an announced plan into a completed event.
3. If the story foregrounds illegality or the original export leg, obtain the Philippine judgment/customs inspection and departure manifest. Otherwise keep that history as attributed context and start the physical trail at the verified Subic consolidation.
